# plan-ad-volume counts every launched ad and reads a hit rate

**Status:** Amended by [ADR-0009](0009-plan-ad-volume-aims-at-a-viable-share-target-not-all-spend.md), 2026-11-16. Accepted 2026-11-16. Amends [ADR-0006](0006-plan-ad-volume-publishes-the-ad-stock-method-in-full.md).

ADR-0006 published the client builds' ad stock model, whose survival curve counts at each age only the ads that spent that day. That curve answers how healthy the ads still running are, which is why a client forecast used it to age today's live ads forward and kept Little's law as a reference only. Read as a lifespan, it inflates W: ads that stop spending or never clear Ramping drop out, so a dry run showed half of new ads below viable by day 2 yet a 38-day Viable lifespan, and 6 new ads a week needed against 36 launched and 8 viable. Every model also counted a launch as one whole viable ad. The skill now counts every Launched ad at every age it has reached, a stopped or Ramping ad being not viable, and splits W into Hit rate (viable at least once in its first 28 days) times Days viable per hit. New ads a week, the catch-up (viable ads short over Hit rate) and the scenarios all divide by the Hit rate, and today's viable ads wear out at 1 over Days viable per hit. Spend per viable ad becomes spend over viable ad-days. The published method now differs from the client forecast marts until those are fixed in `go-crux/analytics`, so a brain run no longer ties to `int_meta__survival_curves`; it checks itself instead (launches a week times W over 7 against today's viable ads). We accept a bigger weekly number and a break with the client marts, because the old figure contradicted the account's own launches.
