# plan-ad-volume scores creative health on fixed published bands

**Status:** Amended by [ADR-0015](0015-plan-ad-volume-counts-a-hit-only-if-its-first-28-day-cac-is-within-1-2x-the-accounts.md), 2026-11-19. Accepted 2026-11-18.

The plan gains a Creative score, after the diagnostic in CTC's Prophit System: the plain mean of seven sub-scores (Evergreen share, Spend degradation, Viable after week 1, Top-5 concentration, Outlier rate, Hit rate, Low-spend rate), each placed on fixed Poor / Average / Good anchors that are the same for every account and published in full in `references/method.md`. This departs from ADR-0009 and ADR-0011, which rejected fixed industry bands for the plan's targets because we cannot defend one number across accounts and a founder believes a target they have already hit. The difference is the job: the Lever best and the Viable share target set what the plan aims at, and stay account-relative; the Creative score only says how healthy the creative mix is against a published yardstick, and a yardstick read against the account itself cannot catch a problem built into it. CTC ranks against its own client base, which we do not have and could not publish, so the anchors are set from CTC's examples and our own accounts, rounded, and are provisional until more accounts run. The score never multiplies the ad count: quality reaches the number only through the Hit rate and Viable spend per hit in the formula, so the score and the plan cannot disagree. Top-10 turnover is shown but not scored, since high turnover is neither good nor bad against Evergreen share, and a CAC-after-launch measure was dropped for Viable after week 1 because order lag and spend moving onto winners make it read as improving.
