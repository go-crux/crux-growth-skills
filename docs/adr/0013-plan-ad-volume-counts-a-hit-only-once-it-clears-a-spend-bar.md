# plan-ad-volume counts a hit only once it clears a spend bar

**Status:** Amended by [ADR-0015](0015-plan-ad-volume-counts-a-hit-only-if-its-first-28-day-cac-is-within-1-2x-the-accounts.md), 2026-11-19. Accepted 2026-11-18. Amends [ADR-0008](0008-plan-ad-volume-counts-every-launched-ad-and-reads-a-hit-rate.md).

Under ADR-0008 a Launched ad was a hit if it was viable on any day in its first 28 days, so an ad that spent £400 and was viable for one day counted the same as one that carried £5,000. A hit must now also reach the Spend bar on its first 28 days: the larger of the 300 lifetime-spend eligibility floor and 3 days of Spend per viable ad. Meta has to have given it budget and it has to have converted, which is CTC's activation test with CAC added. One bar defines both the Hit rate and the Low-spend rate, so no ad is both. A 7-day bar was measured and rejected: it removed about half of one account's hits, left hits and Outliers nearly the same set, pushed the Low-spend rate to about 93% everywhere, and inflated Days viable per hit, since the working days of ads under the bar still sat in the curve's area while the hits they were divided over fell; that would have named the wrong Limiting factor. The bar changes how the curve's area splits between the Hit rate and Days viable per hit, not the area itself, so the ad count barely moves; what changes is that the Hit rate now means an ad that worked at real spend. On small accounts 3 days falls under the eligibility floor and the floor alone sets the bar, which is why it is written as the larger of the two.
