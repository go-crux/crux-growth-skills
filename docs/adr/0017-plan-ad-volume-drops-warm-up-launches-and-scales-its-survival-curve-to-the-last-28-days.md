# plan-ad-volume drops warm-up launches and scales its survival curve to the last 28 days

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-19. Amends [ADR-0008](0008-plan-ad-volume-counts-every-launched-ad-and-reads-a-hit-rate.md) (the Launched ad and the Viable lifespan).

ADR-0008 counted as a Launched ad every ad whose first spend fell after the window's first day, and read the Viable lifespan as the area under the curve those ads draw. Two dry runs on real brains then failed the Self-check, which set the Current pipeline times the Viable lifespan against today's viable ads, and the page explained the gap with a cause it had made up (working ads launched before the window). Probes found almost no viable ads launched before the window, and three real causes. One day is noisy: the daily count of viable ads swings widely from week to week, so a single-day check fails even when the curve is right on average. Ads launched in the window's first 6 weeks are judged before the weekday index, life spend and Peak CAC warm up, so they read far worse than later cohorts, and the viable share already leaves those weeks out while the curve did not. And recent ads can differ from the year's average: on one brain the viable days per ad in their first 28 rose every quarter, so a year-pooled curve tracked actual counts until about two months ago and then ran well under them, which overstates the Steady-state number.

A Launched ad now first spends on or after the window's first day plus 42 days, the same 6 weeks the viable share leaves out, so the curve, Spend per viable ad, Hit rate, Low-spend rate, Outlier rate, Viable after week 1 and the Lever bests all follow. The plan also scales the curve to the last 28 complete days. The actual is the mean daily count of viable Launched ads, the predicted is the mean daily sum, over the Launched ads that have started, of the curve's survival rate at their age that day (ages past the curve read 0), and the Recent fit c is the actual over the predicted, held between 0.5 and 2 and 1 where the predicted is 0. The Viable lifespan is the curve's sum times c. The Hit rate stays as measured, so Days viable per hit, Viable spend per hit, the Steady-state number, `ads_for_spend`, the Roadmap, the slider and the Days a hit works Lever's Now carry c, while the Lever bests and the survival curve chart stay as measured. The Self-check reads the two 28-day means before the fit, passes within 25% of the actual and reports in This run; where c was held, the page says the recent ads differ from the year's by more than the plan will adjust for. It never explains a failed check beyond its own words.

The Current pipeline and Top-10 turnover keep their own month and quarter rules. The Current pipeline's last 3 complete months start after the 6 weeks on a window of 180 days or more. Top-10 turnover counts an ad as new in a quarter by its first spend, so a warm-up launch still counts there though it is not a Launched ad. The Launches best counts Launched ads by month and can read low for a first complete month that starts inside the 6 weeks; a lower best is the cautious error, and the Hit rate best already leaves those months out. We accept a smaller Launched-ad base on a short window, and a plan that follows the last 28 days rather than the year, because the plan is for the ads the account has now and a check that passes for a reason a reader can see is worth more than a curve averaged over ads the account no longer launches.
