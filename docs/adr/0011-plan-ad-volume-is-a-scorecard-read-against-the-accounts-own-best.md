# plan-ad-volume is a scorecard, and names the limiting factor against the account's own best

**Status:** Accepted 2026-11-17. Supersedes [ADR-0010](0010-plan-ad-volume-names-a-focus-from-the-hit-rate.md).

The Focus rule of ADR-0010 misfired on its first dry run: an account needing about half as many launches again sat just over the 1.5 trigger with a sub-15% hit rate, so the page said to work on the hit rate when the gap was a few viable ads. The deeper fault is in the maths: today's viable ads are launches a week times Hit rate times Days viable per hit over 7, so closing a gap by any one Lever alone takes the same multiple, and the size of the ask can never say which Lever is short. Only each Lever's room against what the account has already done can. The skill now reads each Lever against its Lever best (the best rolling 8 weeks of launches, the 90th-percentile launch month's Hit rate, the best mature launch quarter's Days viable per hit) and names a Limiting factor: On track, Volume (the launches needed are within 1.2 times the best), Both (they are at the best Hit rate), else Finding winners or Keeping winners by which has more room. Fixed industry bands were rejected: we cannot defend them across accounts, and a founder believes a target they have hit before. The output becomes a Scorecard: three answer cards and three charted panels (enough ads at today, 1.5 and 2 times spend; the Levers; where spend goes against the target, with the customers On the table), labels only; the Week-by-week plan, the curve, the figures and the method move to How it's worked out, and the Waterfall and the hit-rate scenarios go. Improving now counts as viable in this skill, because it converts like Healthy; audit-ads keeps its narrower meaning, so the shared classification block is untouched and the widening sits outside it. Today's viable ads are read on the last complete day, after a part-loaded day read none. On the table assumes the CAC gap between viable and other spend holds as the share moves, and says so.
